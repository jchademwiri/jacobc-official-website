A 180-page municipal or SOE bid document rewards a slow, literal first read. The clauses that disqualify submissions are rarely hidden in fine print—they are usually sitting in plain sight inside the Special Conditions of Contract or the Scope of Work, where rushed teams skim right past them.

## 1. Start with the closing date, briefing, and CIDB/CSD gates

Before investing 40 hours into pricing and methodology, verify the hard gatekeepers on the cover pages and Invitation to Bid (MBD 1 / SBD 1):
- **Compulsory Briefing Session:** Is attendance mandatory, and must the attendance register be signed by a specific representative?
- **CIDB Grading & Joint Venture Rules:** Does the tender require a specific CIDB class and grade (e.g., `6CE` or `4GB`), and are JV calculation rules triggered if two companies partner?
- **Submission Format:** Is it physical tender-box only, electronic portal, or two-envelope (technical separate from financial)? Submitting pricing inside the technical envelope in a two-envelope bid is an instant disqualification.

## 2. Separate mandatory disqualifiers from scored criteria

Every clause in an RFP falls into one of two buckets, and confusing them wastes time:
- **Mandatory Requirements (Elimination Gates):** Missing these eliminates your bid outright—things like tax compliance pins, CSD registration, signed MBD 4/6.1/8/9 forms, certified ID copies, or proof of vehicle/plant ownership.
- **Scored Criteria (Functionality Points):** These determine your technical score out of 100 before price and specific goals are calculated.

Triage the document into these two lists *before* drafting a single response. Mandatory items get 100% completion first; scored items get prioritized by point weight.

## 3. Annotate Special Conditions and operational SLAs inline

Flag every operational obligation that affects cost or risk while you read:
- **Response Times & Penalties:** If a water tanker or plant hire contract requires mobilization within 4 hours of call-out with penalty deductions for late arrival, that directly affects your fleet staging and backup plan.
- **Statutory Inspections:** Check for Driven Machinery Regulations (DMR 19) load-test requirements, SANS water-quality standards, or hazardous waste disposal manifests.
- **Payment Terms & Invoicing Proof:** Note what supporting documents (signed daily logsheets, weighbridge slips, GPS telemetry) must accompany each monthly invoice so your accounts receivable team can bill cleanly once awarded.

## 4. Never reconstruct requirements from memory

When you finish reading, your annotated PDF or physical binder should feed directly into a structured **Compliance & Responsibility Matrix** with deadlines for every certificate, signature, and pricing schedule. Good tender management is simply making sure nothing relies on memory.
