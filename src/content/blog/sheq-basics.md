If Safety, Health, Environment, and Quality (SHEQ) responsibilities land on your desk as a Tender, Project, or Operations Manager without a dedicated safety background, the terminology can feel intimidating at first. Underneath the acronyms, however, SHEQ is simply structured operational common sense.

## 1. SHEQ is one integrated umbrella over three core standards

In practice, SHEQ brings together:
- **Quality (ISO 9001):** Delivering what the client contracted for, on time, with accurate documentation and billing.
- **Environment (ISO 14001):** Controlling spills, waste disposal, dust, and environmental impacts on site.
- **Occupational Health & Safety (ISO 45001 + OHS Act):** Making sure operators, drivers, and site teams go home unharmed.

## 2. HIRA: Hazard Identification and Risk Assessment

A **HIRA** is the foundation of every safety file and site inspection. Before deploying a crane, excavator, water tanker, or sanitation crew:
1. List every task step (e.g., *offloading machine from lowbed*).
2. Identify the **Hazard** (what can cause harm—e.g., *pinch points, overhead power lines, unstable ground*).
3. Assess the **Risk** (likelihood × severity).
4. Document the **Control** (spotter assigned, ground stability verified, barricading, PPE worn).

Evaluators and safety officers check HIRAs because they prove you thought through site risks *before* mobilizing.

## 3. Statutory compliance for plant & lifting operations (DMR 19)

If your company operates cranes, forklifts, telehandlers, or lifting tackle in South Africa, **Driven Machinery Regulations (DMR 19)** under the OHS Act are non-negotiable:
- Lifting machinery must be registered and inspected by an accredited **Lifting Machinery Entity (LME)** and **Lifting Machinery Inspector (LMI)**.
- Load-test certificates (annual) and tackle inspections (quarterly/6-monthly) must be current and carried with the machine.
- Operators must hold valid, machine-specific competency certificates and medical fitness certificates.

## 4. Non-conformances are system inputs, not blame

One of the biggest mindset shifts in ISO/SHEQ is understanding a **Non-Conformance Report (NCR)**. An NCR isn't a punishment—it is the mechanism that catches a gap (an overdue service, a missing PPE item, a late delivery), investigates *why* it happened, and puts a corrective action in place so it doesn't repeat.

When you treat SHEQ as part of daily project coordination rather than a separate paperwork chore, audits become routine and your tender safety files are always ready.
