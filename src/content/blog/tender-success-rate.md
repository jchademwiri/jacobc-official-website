After years managing municipal and corporate tender submissions—including multi-year City of Tshwane fleet, water tanker, sanitation, and horticulture awards—a handful of habits consistently separate winning bids from losing ones. Most of it comes down to compliance discipline and operational foresight, not last-minute creativity.

## 1. Read the administrative compliance schedule twice

The majority of South African public-sector bids never reach the pricing or preference evaluation stage. They are eliminated during **Stage 1: Administrative Compliance** over preventable errors: an unsigned MBD 4 declaration of interest, a missing initial on a correction, an expired COIDA Letter of Good Standing, or a mismatched company registration number between CSD and the bid form.

Before writing a single paragraph of your technical proposal, build a line-by-line **Compliance Matrix** directly from the tender's List of Returnables. Assign an owner and a verification date to every document.

## 2. Treat functionality scoring as a mathematical threshold

In municipal and SOE tenders, **functionality (technical) evaluation** is a pass/fail gate—typically requiring 70% or 75% before your price and B-BBEE / PPPFA points (80/20 or 90/10) are even opened.

Map every single point on the functionality scorecard:
- If 20 points require signed reference letters on client letterheads with contract values, verify that your letters actually state the values and dates.
- If 15 points require certified copies of operator competencies or vehicle registration papers, never submit uncertified scans or expired certificates.
- Never assume an evaluator will "infer" experience that isn't explicitly documented in the format requested.

## 3. Keep your ISO & SHEQ documentation audit-ready year-round

Waiting until a tender drops to assemble a SHEQ file, baseline HIRA, or quality policy is how teams miss deadlines. When your organisation runs a living **Integrated Management System (ISO 9001, ISO 14001, and ISO 45001)**, your quality manual, environmental spill procedures, safety policies, and DMR 19 lifting equipment registers are already current and signed. Attaching them takes minutes instead of days, and immediately signals operational maturity to the Bid Evaluation Committee.

## 4. Price from operational reality, not guesswork

A tender you win at a loss—or a tender priced without understanding post-award billing rules—becomes an operational liability. Build your rate schedule alongside the people who will actually run and bill the contract:
- Factor in standing time, lowbed mobilization, fuel escalation, and preventative maintenance across a 36-month term.
- Check how invoicing works (e.g., hourly vs. daily vs. per-kilometre) so your rate schedule aligns cleanly with downstream **SAP or Sage** Purchase Order billing.

## 5. Run a 48-hour quality gate before the tender box closes

Never bind a tender on the morning of submission. Lock your content 48 hours ahead of closing so a second pair of eyes can verify:
1. Every page is numbered and indexed according to the tender's instructions.
2. All compulsory signatures, commissioner of oaths stamps, and witnesses are present in wet ink where required.
3. Physical delivery logistics account for traffic, municipal security queues, and tender box slot dimensions.

Winning tenders consistently isn't luck—it is a repeatable compliance system.
